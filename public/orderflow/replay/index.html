How to read the columns

Ladder. Blue BID and red ASK show resting orders in the latest snapshot. The two lighter columns next to the price show recorded trades in the selected millisecond. The trace holds the last amount at each level for 2 seconds of replay time. The amount stays visible while its background fades.

Book memory. Each column is one L2 snapshot. The heading shows its UTC time. The arrow and colour indicate the side. L2 Δ compares the net BID minus ASK at that price with the previous snapshot. The cause of a change between snapshots is unknown.

Trade trace. Each column shows buys minus sells at that price in a separate 5-second window, oldest first. The bands on the right sum signed USD amounts for the selected millisecond. Select a band cell to see its trades on the tape.

Vertical bars. L2 shows each side’s share of the total USD value in the latest snapshot, up to 20 levels on each side. The 120 s meter shows the buy and sell shares of recorded turnover. The numbers next to the colours show the shares. Cell bar length grows linearly within each measure.

Replay. The cursor marks the time of the current event. Events in the same millisecond form one frame. Playback preserves the time between events. You can scrub or step through events. “Show example” opens the saved moment for the current layout. Selecting a cell pauses playback. “Follow price” selects the level with the largest turnover in the next millisecond during playback. If there is only an L2 snapshot, it uses the last trade.

Symbols. “·” means no recorded activity. “—” means that the level or time is outside coverage. A zero delta means buys and sells balance. Market delta requires the full 120 s. Tape coverage shows how much time was recorded in the window. With partial coverage, amounts and shares include only the trades received. L2 age is the time from the most recent snapshot to the cursor.

Controls. On the ladder, ↑ and ↓ change the price, ← and → change the time, and Space plays or pauses. Escape clears the band filter. The animation toggle respects your device’s reduced-motion setting.

Data sources. BTC is from the versioned public HyperQuant archive. Gold and the S&P 500 are from a local recording of public Hyperliquid channels on 20 Nov 2026. Each example covers two minutes of replay. The USD value is price multiplied by quantity. The trade source provides the side. The engine uses integer millionths of a US dollar.
